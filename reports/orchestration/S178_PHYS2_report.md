## 2. S177 open item (a): the 197 events lifted above 150 C

### 2.1 Diagnosis

The S177 cap rule never sets the evaporation cap below a temperature the text states for an aqueous liquid. 520 events were lifted, 197 of them above 150 C (FOODCOM 191, GUTENBERG_SEGMENT 4, WIKIBOOKS 2). The cause is in Layer 1, as suspected: `condition_binding` 0.1.0 bound to each heat event the temperature **nearest to its verb among all temperatures of the sentence**. A joined step such as "boil the potatoes ... then roast at 400 F" gave the boil the 400 F (204 C) of the roast; a joined step with no punctuation ("preheat the oven to 375 F" followed by "cook the pasta in boiling water") gave the boil the oven temperature; a braise in a named oven at 325 F gave the braise (a LIQUID_PARTIAL medium) the oven temperature (synthetic examples; the corpus sentences are not quoted in any committed file). Reading the 689 aqueous-medium events of the three families whose Layer 1 temperature was text-stated above 100 C (221 above 150 C; agent read of the sentences, not quoted here) the stated value above 150 C was an oven, grill or stove set-point attached to a liquid verb (the sentence has an oven, bake, roast, broil or grill word in 181 of the 221), or a food target temperature ("until a thermometer reads 165 F") taken as a bath temperature; the 40 without such a word are mostly candy and caramel stages and a few set-points stated without any cue word. The corpus also has the opposite, legitimate case: sugar-syrup and jam stages ("boil to 240 F, soft ball stage"; 105-157 C), for which the S177 rule was written and which must stay.

### 2.2 The rule (Layer 1 0.2.0, tests `tests/unit/test_s178_phys2_l1.py`)

1. **Ownership.** A temperature belongs to one event: the last temperature-owning event (heat, heat control, cooling, preservation, ferment, resting, storage) whose verb precedes it in the sentence, else the first that follows; coordinated verbs ("bake (braise) at 350") share it; a second temperature of one verb that leads the next owner-less verb ("turn the oven down to 350 and bake") goes to that verb. A heat event may also use a temperature owned by an earlier setter (preheat / heat control / generic heat / the verb reduce: "heat the oil to 350 F, then fry") or by an earlier oven step of the same sentence ("bake at 350 for 30 minutes, uncover and bake 15 more").
2. **Not a liquid temperature.** A temperature with an oven, grill, bake, roast, preheat or heat-level cue next to it (cue window bounded by clause breaks), or an oil cue when shared from an earlier "heat the oil", is never the bath temperature of an aqueous step. It is kept as the separate input `ambient_temperature` (stated, with span), flagged `OVEN_OR_AMBIENT_TEMPERATURE_STATED_NOT_USED_AS_LIQUID_T` in Layer 2.
3. **Food targets are endpoints.** A temperature after a thermometer, probe, internal / core temperature, reads or registers cue (and the later temperatures of the same list) is a target of the food, stored in the `endpoint` block, not a medium temperature. A sugar-syrup temperature stays a liquid temperature ("boil to 240 F", candy thermometer: the sugar-syrup words block the diversion).
4. **Audit trail.** `temperature_note` on each changed event records the 0.1.0 candidate span and why it moved (`OWNED_BY_OTHER_EVENT`, `OVEN_OIL_OR_HEAT_LEVEL_CUE_TEMPERATURE_OF_LIQUID_STEP`, `TARGET_FOOD_TEMPERATURE`).

An agent read of about 160 changed events (all 94 WIKIBOOKS notes of the first version and about 70 FOODCOM events) found five systematic mistakes of earlier versions, each fixed with a test before the final run: a second oven step losing the oven temperature, "reads at least 170 F", "registers 375 F on a deep-fat thermometer" (the oil), a list of target temperatures, and the sugar-syrup exception. The final rule was re-read on the same FOODCOM sample with no clearly wrong case left; this is an agent read, not a precision measurement (T4-C26 is the audited one).
